## Guidelines

A popover borrows the user's attention without taking the page from them. Use it
when the next step is small enough to finish in place.

### When to use

Use a popover when **all** of these hold:

- The content is interactive — something to press, type in, or choose
- It belongs to a specific element on the page, and that relationship matters
- It is short enough to finish without scrolling
- Losing it to a stray click outside would cost the user nothing important

If the last point fails, the work deserves a `Dialog`. An outside press
dismisses a popover with no confirmation.

### Choosing between overlays

| Component | Content | Focus | Anchored |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Tooltip** | Short, non-interactive | Stays on trigger | Yes |
| **Popover** | Interactive, small | Moves into overlay | Yes |
| **Dialog** | Interactive, any size | Moves into overlay | No |
| **Drawer** | Interactive, large | Moves into overlay | No |

The most common mistake is using a popover for a text hint. Tooltips open on
hover and focus and leave focus alone; a popover requires a press and pulls focus
in. For a hint, that is friction with no benefit.

The second most common is using a popover for a destructive confirmation. It can
be dismissed by clicking anywhere else, which is the opposite of what a
confirmation needs.

### Best practices

> [!TIP]\
> **Do** name every popover. Give `Popover.Content` an `aria-label`, or point
> `aria-labelledby` at a heading inside it. An unnamed dialog is announced as
> just that.

> [!TIP]\
> **Do** let the trigger describe what opens. "Filters" tells the user what to
> expect; an unlabelled icon does not.

> [!CAUTION]\
> **Do not** stack a popover on a popover. Nested overlays are hard to dismiss
> predictably and hard to follow with a screen reader. Anchor both to the same
> surface, or promote the second step to a `Dialog`.

> [!CAUTION]\
> **Do not** hide the only route to a task inside a popover. Anything a user must
> be able to find should be reachable without discovering a trigger first.

> [!TIP]\
> **Do** constrain the width when the content is prose. Text that stretches the
> full surface is harder to read than text with a sensible measure.

### Content

Keep the content to one job. A popover that has grown a header, several sections
and a scrollbar has outgrown the pattern.

Order the content the way it will be used: what the user came for first, and the
action that closes the popover last. Where an action dismisses the popover, say
what it does — "Apply filters" rather than "OK".

### Placement

Let the default `bottom` placement stand unless there is a reason not to. It is
predictable, and it is where users look after pressing something.

Change it when the trigger sits near an edge and a different side keeps the
content closer to where the user is working. Placement is a preference either
way: the popover flips and shifts to stay on screen, so it may not honour the
side you asked for.

Use `offset` to adjust the gap, not to nudge the surface into an alignment that
`placement` already expresses.
